# Dead Cells (Motion Twin / Evil Empire, 2017 EA, 2018 release onward): Meta Progression, Economy and Difficulty

Version context: Early Access May 2017, 1.0 release on 7 Aug 2018 ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/dead-cells/hero-development-and-cells/z49ebd)). Key progression-relevant updates: 1.1 (PC 22 Dec 2018: Custom Mode, residual-cell bag, Forge rework), 1.2 (5 BSC added, Rise of the Giant era), 1.9 "Update of Plenty" (blueprint drop cap per biome), 2.3 "Whack-a-Mole" (30 Mar 2021: BSC curve rework, auto blueprint turn-in, Collector's Intern), 2.9 "Breaking Barriers" (Assist Mode), 3.0 (removed the stat bonus from Hunter's Grenade; Random Melee Weapon requirement lowered), wiki currently tracks v3.5. Most numbers below come from deadcells.wiki.gg (community wiki, current-version), not from developer documents. Treat them as current-version values. Older guides often show different numbers.

## Cells: drops, per-run yield, loss on death, the Collector

### Takeaway
Cells are the single meta currency. Enemies drop them at random. They are spent at the Collector in the "Passages" between biomes and are **all lost on death if unspent**. Higher BSC multiplies enemy cell drops (x2 at 2-3 BSC, x3 at 4-5 BSC). I found no reliable per-enemy drop rate. Guides put a fast early-game farming run at about 120-260 cells.

### Cited Findings
- Cells are lost on death. A cell you walk away from, or leave behind in a room, cannot be collected, but you can pick it up again by coming back near it. — [deadcells.wiki.gg, Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells)
- Cells are spent in two places: the Collector (item and upgrade unlocks) and the Blacksmith/Legendary Forge (higher chance of better item quality). — [wiki Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells)
- **Residual Cells bag:** when you reach the Throne Room (beat the game), the cells you still hold, plus **40 cells dropped by the Hand of the King**, go into a bag that appears at the start of your next run. If you don't pick it up, or reset the run, those cells are lost. The bag was added in v1.1 ("Unused cells carry over in a bag to the start of your next run"). — [wiki Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells); [wiki 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1)
- **BSC cell multiplier:** enemy cell drops x2 at 2 BSC and 3 BSC, x3 at 4 BSC and 5 BSC. — [wiki Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells). Community guide summary: "0, 2 and 4 boss stem cells ... give regular, double and triple cell drops." — [Naguide via search](https://naguide.com/?p=1270)
- Liposuction pickup: 0.3% drop chance from enemy kills. For 45 s it gives +1 cell per kill (paid as gold). — [wiki Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells)
- Clone Elites: when an elite clones itself, killing one copy "will drop cells usually," and the other drops the item(s). — [wiki Enemies](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Enemies)
- **Per-run yield (third-party farming guide, Aug 2018, launch version):** speed route Promenade → Ossuary → Black Bridge → Stilt Village → Forgotten Sepulcher → Clock Room gives "at least 260 cells" in under 30 minutes. A shortened route to Black Bridge gives about 120 cells in 15 min. A full-clear standard route gives "50 or more cells per area" at the lowest difficulty, with cells mostly flowing from tier 5-6 biomes onward. — [GamesHedge](https://www.gameshedge.com/dead-cells-cell-farming-guide/)
- **Launch-era Collector door:** at launch (Aug 2018), the exit door of the safe room "won't open until you've spent every cell you've collected," but players could break the door down and carry cells forward, at the risk of "losing everything." — [Destructoid, 15 Aug 2018](https://destructoid.com/?p=235925)
- The Collector sits in his laboratory in the Passages between each pair of biomes. He exchanges cells and blueprints for unlocks that persist across runs. Upgrade categories are General improvements, Mutations, Weapons and Skills. Outfits are bought from the Tailor. — [wiki The Collector](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Collector)
- At 5 BSC the **Collector's Intern** replaces the Collector in the Passages and works the same way. The real Collector appears only at the Observatory (final biome). The Intern arrived in 2.3 as a "community-suggested addition." — [wiki The Collector](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Collector); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- Gamepressure (Aug 2018) says the first stage of an upgrade costs 5-15 cells, and later upgrades "can cost dozens or even hundreds." All unused cells are lost permanently on death. — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/dead-cells/hero-development-and-cells/z49ebd)

### Inferences
- Losing cells on death plus spend points between biomes create a "bank it or risk it" decision at every Passage. The launch-version door made banking close to mandatory. Today players can carry cells forward. Carrying cells only pays off for big Forge investments or to reach the end-of-run bag, so the loss acts as a tax on hoarding rather than a punishment for dying.
- The BSC multiplier makes higher difficulties the efficient farm. Late-game players are paid more for playing harder content, which keeps the Forge's 13.5k-cell sink reachable.

### Gaps
- No source found giving exact per-enemy, per-elite or per-boss cell drop counts or probabilities. The wiki Cells and Enemies pages don't list them.
- No reliable figure for "cells per typical full run at 0 BSC". The 120-260 figures are launch-era speed-farm routes, not average runs.
- Whether unspent cells are still lost at any point other than death (e.g., when leaving the Passage) in the current version is not stated explicitly. Destructoid and the wiki imply they carry over.

## Blueprints: count, drop sources and rates, cell costs

### Takeaway
About 300 blueprints are listed: 256 enemy/boss-droppable, 42 secret and 2 from a 5 BSC boss. Drop chance is 100%, 10%, 1.7%, 0.4% or 0.03% depending on rarity tier. A blueprint must be carried to a Passage alive (auto turn-in since 2.3) and then bought with cells. Individual unlock costs seen in sources run from about 10-40 cells for cheap items to 100+ for weapons, and up to 2,000 for some outfits. No source totals the cost of unlocking everything.

### Cited Findings
- **Counts by drop-rarity tier (current wiki):** Always 104, Common 33, Uncommon 53, Rare 64, Legendary 2 = 256 enemy/boss blueprints. Plus 42 secret blueprints, plus 2 from the 5 BSC-exclusive boss that need a secret condition (about 300 total, though the wiki gives no headline total). 13 "Override Limit" blueprints can drop alongside another blueprint. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)
- **Drop rates:** Always 100%, Common 10%, Uncommon 1.7%, Rare 0.4%, Legendary 0.03%. "Rare/Legendary" here is a blueprint label and has nothing to do with Legendary gear. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)
- **Blueprints by the BSC level where they first become available:** Normal 51/19/29/47/2 (Always/Common/Uncommon/Rare/Legendary). Hard 9/0/2/5/0. Very Hard 9/6/1/4/0. Expert 9/0/1/5/0. Nightmare 9/0/2/3/0. Hell 3/1/0/0/0. Most (148) are available at 0 BSC. About 66 are gated behind 1-5 BSC. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)
- **Sources:** each enemy-dropped blueprint is assigned to a specific enemy at level generation. Boss blueprints are tied to a minimum BSC and drop in a fixed order. Killing a boss on a higher difficulty does not add drops. Secret blueprints sit behind hidden routes, keys, rune gates, puzzles and timed doors, and some need higher difficulty or specific items. Some secret blueprints are rewards for completing a number of Daily Runs. Hunter's Grenade turns an enemy into an elite, and the Blueprint Extractor then drops a random blueprint from that enemy's remaining pool. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)
- **Per-biome cap:** v1.9 limited enemies to at most one blueprint drop per biome. v2.3 changed this to one outfit plus one other item per biome. Secret-area and Hunter's Grenade blueprints are exempt. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- **Death:** a blueprint is lost if you die before delivering it. Since 2.3, blueprints are turned in automatically at biome transitions, so you no longer need to visit the Collector to bank them. Since 2.3, items obtained from completing a blueprint are Colorless. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- **Hunter's Mirror** (150 cells, needs Specialist's Showroom) shows a random enemy or boss that still carries an unacquired blueprint. This is the developers' tool against the long tail of 0.4%/0.03% drops. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades); [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)
- **Example unlock costs (third-party, version not always stated):** Scythe Claws 100, Boomerang 100, Corrupted Power 80, Tesla Coil 40, Emergency Door 42 (Steam forum), Radiant Triumph outfit 2,000. — [search summary of Gamepressure/Twinfinite/Steam pages](https://www.gamepressure.com/dead-cells/what-uprgrades-should-be-unlocked-first/z0b356); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/5/2263565217510453018)
- Melee weapons: 70 table entries (69 distinct). 5 are always unlocked: Rusty Sword, Balanced Blade, Twin Daggers, Nutcracker, Transformation. The wiki weapon table has no cell-cost column. — [wiki Melee weapons](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Melee_weapons)
- Achievement milestones show how far players get: "Unlock 10 weapons" 70.5% of Steam players, "Unlock 10 skills" 45.8%. — [Steam global achievements](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/588650/achievements)
- **Outfits:** found as separate blueprints (one per biome cap since 2.3) and bought from the Tailor, not the Collector. Some cost thousands of cells (Radiant Triumph 2,000). Outfits are cosmetic. — [wiki The Collector](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Collector); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)

### Inferences
- Two-key structure: a blueprint is the "find" gate (RNG plus exploration plus a survival check) and cells are the "pay" gate. Rarity tiers spread blueprint discovery across hundreds of hours, while the Always-tier (104) keeps early runs rewarding.
- About 22% of blueprints are BSC-gated, so the difficulty ladder doubles as the late item-pool expansion.

### Gaps
- No complete cost table or total "cells to unlock everything" was found. It would need summing the costs on each item's wiki page.
- No breakdown of blueprint count by category (weapons/skills/mutations/outfits) was found on the Blueprints page. Item counts are per category page and change with each DLC.

## Permanent upgrades: flasks, gold retention, recycling, forge, Custom Mode, runes

### Takeaway
Permanent power is deliberately small and capped. Flasks go to 4 charges for 505 cells total. Gold Reserves keep 500-2,500 gold for 740 cells. Recycling converts 7-15% of sale value. The big cell sink is the Legendary Forge: 13,500 cells to max all tiers, and that only raises item-quality odds. There are no HP or damage stat upgrades. Runes are permanent movement and traversal keys that open alternate biome routes.

### Cited Findings
- **Health Flask (current wiki):** I = 5 cells (1 use, heals 60% max HP over a few seconds, removes 150 Malaise). II = 50 (2 uses). III = 150, needs 10 items unlocked (3 uses). IV = 300, needs 25 items (4 uses). Total 505 cells for 4 charges. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- **Gold Reserves (gold kept on death):** I 5 cells → 500 gold. II 35 → 1,000. III 100 (8 items) → 1,500. IV 200 (15 items) → 2,000. V 400 (30 items) → 2,500. The V blueprint is in the Ancient Sewers secret maze and needs the Homunculus Rune. Total 740 cells. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- **Recycling:** I 25 cells converts dropped or unwanted objects into gold at 7% of sale price. II 120 cells raises that to 15%. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- **Starting loadout:** Random Melee Weapon / Random Starter Bow / Random Starter Shield cost 65 cells each and need 2 items unlocked. 3.0 lowered the melee requirement from 12 items to 2. Recycling Tubes cost 150 cells (blueprint from the Hand of the King on 1 BSC) and offer a choice of 4 starting equipment sets. Backpack costs 100 cells (15 items) and carries one spare weapon. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- **Shop/run-shaping upgrades:** Restock 300 (20 items; 5 uses per shop, +40% price each use). Specialist's Showroom 150 (20 items). Hunter's Mirror 150. Merchandise Categories 300 (30 items + Graveyard blueprint + Architect's Key). Advanced Forge I 200 (needs Gold Reserves IV, 40 items, and a blueprint from Conjunctivius's 6th defeat) lets forge quality upgrades be applied twice. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- **Legendary Forge (item-quality odds), current:** "+" tier costs 500 cells (+5% per 25 cells). "++" tier costs 3,000 (+5% per 150). "S" tier costs 10,000 (+5% per 500). Each tier must reach 100% before the next opens. Total 13,500 cells. — [wiki Legendary Forge](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Legendary_Forge)
- **Version difference:** at launch (Aug 2018) Destructoid reported the three meters needed **12,500** cells combined, against 13,500 on the current wiki. In 1.1, Forge upgrades got a flat price. — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=235925); [wiki 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1)
- **Forge caps by BSC:** at 1 BSC "++" can reach 100%. 2 BSC allows S up to 25%, 3 BSC up to 50%, 4-5 BSC up to 100%. Base (0 BSC) caps aren't cleanly stated, but the table implies + 100%, ++ 50%, S not investable. — [wiki Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells); [wiki Legendary Forge](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Legendary_Forge)
- **Runes (10: 8 base, 2 Richter Mode):** Vine (Promenade, elite archer; opens Toxic Sewers and more). Customization (Ramparts; unlocks Custom Mode). Challenger (Black Bridge, Concierge; Daily Challenge). Teleportation (Toxic Sewers elite; teleport monoliths). Ram (Ossuary elite; break floors, opens Ancient Sewers). Explorer's (Forgotten Sepulcher; map reveal after 80% exploration, opens no route). Spider (Slumbering Sanctuary elite; wall clinging, opens Prison Depths, Corrupted Prison, Slumbering Sanctuary and Graveyard). Homunculus (Throne Room, Hand of the King; Cavern access and remote grab). Richter Mode adds Dash and High Jump. — [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)
- Rune achievement rates (Steam): Vine 87.3%, Challenger 72.8%, Teleportation 64.4%, Ram 45.4%. Beat the Hand of the King 40.3%. — [Steam achievements](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/588650/achievements)
- **Custom Mode:** introduced in 1.1 (PC 22 Dec 2018; consoles 14 Feb 2019; launched on mobile without it). Unlocked via the Customization Rune in Ramparts (since 1.2). It limits the item, mutation and outfit pools, sets starting equipment, and toggles meta upgrades (max potions 0-4, Gold Reserves 0-5, Recycling 0-2, Forge none/Advanced). It has 21 misc modifiers (fixed seed, BSC, curse level, etc.), 4 Malaise modifiers and 11 gameplay modifiers. Enabling fewer than 20 items in the Equipment pool disables achievements (mutations don't count). Many gameplay and Malaise modifiers also disable achievements. Items unlocked in Custom Mode carry over to Normal Mode. Only items unlocked at the Collector can be toggled. — [wiki Custom Mode](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Custom_Mode); [wiki 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1)
- 2.3 added a note to Custom Mode that items "can be locked back in the Collector UI." This is the pool-locking mechanism for normal play. — [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- **Assist Mode (2.9 "Breaking Barriers"):** continues (0/1/3/7/infinite), auto-hit, easier parry, slower traps, map reveal, trap damage down to 20%, enemy HP and damage down to 20% (2.5% steps). It does **not** disable achievements or BSC acquisition. It only hides your score from the Daily leaderboard. — [wiki Assist Mode](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Assist_Mode)

### Inferences
- Total "core" stat-like meta (4 flasks + Gold V + Recycling II) costs about 1,390 cells, which is cheap next to the Forge's 13,500. The design front-loads survival comfort and puts the long-tail sink in odds-shifting (quality) rather than raw stats.
- Many upgrades require N items unlocked (10/25/30/40), which forces players to buy blueprints and so widens the item pool. This is the root of the dilution complaint below.

### Gaps
- No developer-sourced list of original (1.0) upgrade prices to compare against current ones. The wiki version history records no cell-cost changes except the 3.0 item-count requirement.

## Boss Stem Cells (difficulty 0-5)

### Takeaway
BSCs are earned one at a time by beating a final boss (Hand of the King, the Queen, or Dracula Final Form) with N BSC active. The 5th comes from beating the Giant at 4 BSC. Each level strips healing (fountains to flask refills to nothing), changes early boss phases, raises item level, multiplies cells, unlocks blueprints, doors and scroll fragments, and at 5 BSC adds Malaise. There is no reliable time-to-5BC figure. Anecdotes range from tens to 100+ hours.

### Cited Findings
- **Current per-level effects (wiki):**
  - 0 BSC: base difficulty, health fountains in every Passage (per the 2.3 notes).
  - 1 BSC (Hard): early boss phases change. A fountain every other Passage, with a flask recharge in the others. Forge ++ cap goes to 100%. New doors (Prison Depths → Ancient Sewers, Corrupted Prison → Ramparts).
  - 2 BSC (Very Hard): no fountains, one flask charge per Passage. S Forge up to 25%. Cell drops x2. New doors to the Cavern and Guardian's Haven.
  - 3 BSC (Expert): 3 flask charges per run (after the 1st boss, before the 2nd, after the Lighthouse). S up to 50%. Cells x2. Scroll Fragments appear. Item level +1. Cursed-chest colorless items go from ++ to S. Door from Ramparts → Insufferable Crypt.
  - 4 BSC (Nightmare): enemies have larger detection range and **teleport to the player**. No flask refills. S up to 100%. Cells x3. More scroll fragments. Item level +3.
  - 5 BSC (Hell): everything from 4 BSC plus **Malaise**. The Collector's Intern replaces the Collector. Unlocks the 7th Astrolab stage.
  — [wiki Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells)
- **Earning:** the first BSC comes from beating the Hand of the King, the Queen or Dracula (Final Form) with 0 BSC. Each further win at N gives N+1. The 5th requires beating the Giant at 4 BSC. Using Aspects prevents acquiring the next BSC. Cells are inserted or removed in the Tube at the start area. — [wiki Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells)
- **History:** introduced in 0.5 (Foundry Update) as "Boss Runes," each dropped by a different boss. 5 BSC added in 1.2. 1.9 removed the legendary stat bonus at 3+ BSC. **2.3 rebalanced the curve** (BC0 fountains everywhere → BC5 no health, teleport and Malaise) and **removed Malaise from 4 BSC**. 2.3 also capped fatal fall damage at 15% max HP and stopped Malaise from boosting enemy movement speed. 3.0 removed stats from Hunter's Grenade. — [wiki Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- 1.1 removed mob auto-scaling (levels now have fixed difficulty) and made money costs scale faster than profits on higher difficulties. — [wiki 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1)
- **Time to 5BC (anecdotal only):** Steam players report about 30-70 h just to beat 1 BSC. One player was stuck about 30 h on 2 BSC, then got 3, 4 and 5 in a single day. HowLongToBeat (cited by PlatPrices) gives Main 14 h, Main+Extras 32 h, Completionist 92 h. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/0/1651045226228400747); [PlatPrices](https://platprices.com/en-us/game/6600-dead-cells)
- Funnel proxy: only 40.3% of Steam owners have beaten the Hand of the King (the first BSC). — [Steam achievements](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/588650/achievements)

### Inferences
- BSC is an opt-in "ascension" ladder whose main lever is **removing healing**, not inflating enemy stats. Teleport (4) and Malaise (5) are behavioural pressure tools against slow and safe play.
- Higher BSC is self-funding (more cells, better Forge caps, +item levels, exclusive blueprints), so it serves as both the endgame and the late meta-unlock layer.

### Gaps
- Steam achievement percentages for 1-5 BSC weren't visible in the fetched achievement page (truncated). No authoritative median hours to 5 BSC was found.

## Item-pool dilution criticism and Motion Twin's response; later progression changes

### Takeaway
Players have complained since EA (2017-2018) that forced unlocks (needed to reach upgrade thresholds and better items) dilute the drop pool with weapons they don't want. Motion Twin answered in three ways: Custom Mode (1.1, Dec 2018) with item pool toggles, Collector-UI re-locking of unlocked items (noted in 2.3), and large balance passes that made weak items viable. Blueprint pacing was also changed (1.9 per-biome cap, 2.3 auto-delivery).

### Cited Findings
- 2017 Steam suggestion: an NPC that, "for a price, can remove some objects from the item pool for one run." Others feared unlimited removal would make the game too easy. — [Steam discussion (Dec 2017)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/3/1291816880499652302)
- 2018 forum threads ask whether unlocking everything hurts runs by diluting the pool, and complain that players "often have to unlock items unfitting to our playstyle in order to make useful items available." — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/0/1638675549003292971); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/3/1621724915796861021)
- The 1.1 patch notes describe Custom Mode as allowing "unlocking and relocking items" and changing rules. The notes give no explicit dilution rationale. — [wiki 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1)
- 2.3: Custom Mode now notes that items "can be locked back in the Collector UI." — [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3)
- Achievement guardrail: under 20 enabled items in the Equipment pool disables achievements. — [wiki Custom Mode](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Custom_Mode)
- Balance answer: "What's the Damage?" buffed about 70 items so weak ones could compete. Version 1.26 (console numbering) made historically weak items viable. — [search summary of GameSpot/mp1st coverage](https://gamespot.com/articles/dead-cells-update-features-half-life-crossover-ite/1100-6475225/); [mp1st 1.26](https://mp1st.com/news/dead-cells-update-1-26-now-out-for-update-24-this-august-12)
- Progression pacing changes: 1.9 capped enemy blueprint drops at one per biome. 2.3 allowed one outfit plus one item per biome, auto-delivered blueprints, and added the Intern. 3.0 cut the Random Melee Weapon requirement from 12 to 2 items. — [wiki Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints); [wiki 2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3); [wiki Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades)

### Inferences
- The answer was **opt-out curation** (lock items back) rather than making unlocks optional. The 20-item achievement floor shows the developers wanted to stop players from shrinking the pool to a degenerate build set.

### Gaps
- No developer statement found that explicitly names "pool dilution" as the reason for Custom Mode or re-locking. The exact version that added Collector-UI re-locking outside Custom Mode isn't confirmed (only the 2.3 note references it).
- No record found of cell-price changes to individual blueprints across updates.

## Developer statements on why blueprints, cell loss and minimal stat upgrades

### Takeaway
Primary developer rationale on meta progression is thin in accessible sources. The clearest statement is Sébastien Bénard's (lead dev) Jan 2018 PlayStation Blog post: power should track player skill, not inflated character stats. The "metagame" (unlocks of weapons, levels and perks) exists to give a sense of progress when runs end early. I found no GDC talk or formal postmortem on cells or blueprints.

### Cited Findings
- Bénard (Lead Developer, PlayStation Blog, 25 Jan 2018): the aim is to "empower the player, right from the get go." "The challenge is not in acquiring more abilities." The goal is the character's strength "being equal to the extent of your skills," and progress comes "not because we will have made your character super overpowered." The metagame provides "a meaningful sensation of progression" when a run ends early; "That's the role of the metagame." Unlocks are new weapons, levels and perks that give new objectives. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2018/01/25/gorgeous-rogue-lite-action-platformer-dead-cells-is-battling-onto-ps4-this-year)
- Bénard (Game Informer, 30 Dec 2018): "We knew at the beginning we wanted something difficult, but not unfair." On deaths: "You need to know why it was your fault." — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/interview/2018/12/30/dead-cells-designer-discusses-scrapped-ideas-roguelikes-and-the-potential-for)
- Steve Filby (Motion Twin, interview): lists the persistent unlocks as weapon unlocks, gold-kept-on-death upgrades and starting-weapon upgrades, which make the player feel more powerful over time despite restarting. — [Big Shiny Robot](https://bigshinyrobot.com/video-games/interview-steve-filby-motion-twin/) (via search snippet; not fetched in full)
- Filby (PCGamesInsider, 2 May 2018) on procgen fairness: they mixed random and hand-made chunks to avoid runs where "you can see from the very first tile that you're screwed." — [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/indie-interview/66988/how-motion-twin-stopped-dead-cells-being-just-another-roguelike-pixel-art-metroidvania-game/)
- Runes were described in press as Motion Twin's solution to Metroidvania gating in a roguelite: permanent upgrades that open a type of path initially blocked. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Cells)
- Game Developer (17 Aug 2018) covered a Bénard Twitch interview on design and Early Access feedback, but the article reports no specifics on progression. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/sebastien-benard-breaks-down-the-fine-details-of-i-dead-cells-i-design)

### Inferences
- The "strength = skill" principle explains why no HP or damage stat upgrades exist. The meta buys options (items, routes, starting choice), consistency (flasks, gold kept) and odds (Forge), not raw stats. Losing cells on death fits the permadeath framing Bénard calls core ("permadeath, randomness and challenging combat are at the core").

### Gaps
- No primary source found where Motion Twin explicitly explains **why cells are lost on death** or **why blueprints are a separate find-then-pay gate**. A Motion Twin GDC talk and a Game Developer postmortem on this topic were not located. Bénard's personal blog (deepnight.net) and the full Twitch interview were not checked.
