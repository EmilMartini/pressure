# Rebuild Pressure's meta around options, not stats

The three reference games make permanent stat power either small or absent, and put most of their between-run progression into options, agency and opt-in difficulty. Hades keeps its combat-relevant Mirror talents cheap at about 6.4k of 35,365 Darkness and builds them as swappable A/B sidegrades. Dead Cells caps permanent power at 4 flask charges and kept gold, and spends its big currency sink on item-quality odds. Risk of Rain 2 has no stat meta at all: about 205 challenges unlock survivors, skills, items and artifacts. Pressure points the other way. Measured from its code, the meta layer is a 212-node, 326-point tree of +1% to +3% stat nodes, of which 120 points sit in element branches that do nothing until that element is in the pool, plus 55 achievements that open the run with only 4 of 43 guns (9%) and a handful of the 65 reactions. Bot-paced income of 1 point per run puts tree completion at about 286 to 326 runs. The rework this report recommends has four parts. First, fix the code and documentation defects the audit found, which are cheap and fully measured. Second, collapse the stat tree into a small, capped survival layer plus a sidegrade loadout. Third, front-load element breadth, because elements are Pressure's real "fun content". Fourth, add a Heat-style opt-in difficulty ladder with capped rewards. All of this comes with a large caveat: every Pressure number comes from a scripted bot that dies on floor 1. Human pacing, win rates and how players spend points are unknown, so the recommendations are design bets, ranked by how directly the evidence supports them.

## How to read this report: measured data versus opinion

The report uses three tiers of evidence and labels them in the text. **Measured** means read from a source file, a wiki table, a patch note or a Steam achievement API, or arithmetic on those numbers. **Developer statement** means a quoted designer giving intent, not proof of effect. **Inference** means my reading of what the numbers imply, including every recommendation. For the comparison games, numbers come mostly from community wikis (hades.fandom.com, deadcells.wiki.gg, riskofrain2.wiki.gg, vampire.survivors.wiki) and from Steam global achievement percentages pulled on 2026-11-05. Those percentages use all owners as the denominator, so ratios within one game mean more than the raw values, and comparisons across games are confounded by run length, price and audience. For Pressure, every number comes from GDScript at commit f2760ba, 21 telemetry records (all `bot: true`) and 47 autotest logs. No human has ever earned or spent a mastery point ([main.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/main.gd); [runs.jsonl](C:/Users/emilm/AppData/Roaming/Godot/app_userdata/Pressure/telemetry/runs.jsonl)).

## Hades sells survival cheaply and agency expensively

### The Mirror is a loadout of 12 sidegrade pairs, not a ladder

**Measured.** The Mirror of Night has **12 talent pairs (24 talents)**. Only one side of each pair can be active, players can switch sides freely, and each side is ranked separately. Maxing both sides of everything costs **35,365 Darkness** plus **65 Chthonic Keys**, and a full respec costs **1 Key and refunds all Darkness** ([Hades Wiki: Mirror of Night](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Mirror_of_Night)). The cost curve has two halves. Survival is cheap at first: the first Death Defiance costs 30 Darkness, the second and third cost 500 and 1,000, and Thick Skin's +50 max HP costs 625 over 10 ranks. Agency is expensive: the two "Fated" reroll talents cost **21,000 together, about 59% of the total**. Re-adding the wiki's per-rank costs gives a non-boon, non-Fated "combat core" of **6,365 Darkness**, about 18% of the total. The wiki's printed 6,165 appears to be a typo, because 6,365 plus the 8,000 of boon-odds talents equals the wiki's own 14,365 subtotal ([Hades Wiki: Mirror of Night](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Mirror_of_Night)). The pairs make each slot a trade: three Death Defiances or one revive per chamber; +50 HP or +25% damage while above 80% HP; rerolling rooms or rerolling boons.

The structure was a revision, not the launch design. Early Access Patch 040 (March 2020) introduced the flip sides "inviting more choices to fit your playstyle". It also cut the total Darkness cost, replaced one-rank-at-a-time refunds with a full refund, and refunded players who had over-invested ([Supergiant: Nighty Night Update](https://supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-nighty-night-update-patch-notes)). **Developer statement.** Greg Kasavin says the Mirror exists "to make you meaningfully more powerful from one playthrough to a next", combined with "your own growing knowledge" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/features/hades-exclusive-interview-supergiant)). Hades does sell stat power, so it is the closest reference for Pressure's current design. Even so, it keeps that power to a few dozen ranks, cheap at the start, and lets the player trade it for other things.

### Six currencies, each mapped to one sink

**Measured.** Darkness buys the Mirror. Keys unlock Mirror tiers (5/10/20/30 Keys for pairs of talents) and weapons (24 Keys). Gemstones and Diamonds go to the House Contractor (32,585 Gemstones for every upgrade). Nectar and Ambrosia go to relationships. Titan Blood goes to weapon aspects: **306 for all aspects** ([Hades Wiki: Darkness](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Darkness); [Chthonic Key](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chthonic_Key); [Gemstones](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Gemstones); [Titan Blood](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Titan_Blood)). The rare currencies come from bosses. The first kill with each weapon pays one; after that they come only as **per-weapon, per-Heat bounties up to Heat 20**, which is 240 Titan Blood and 120 Diamonds in total, so collecting everything takes 21 successful clears per weapon ([Hades Wiki: Pact of Punishment](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment); [Diamond](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Diamond)). When a boss pays no bounty it drops Darkness instead, 550 across a full clear ([Hades Wiki: Darkness](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Darkness)). No reliable figure exists for runs or hours to max the Mirror. **Inference.** Mapping each currency to one sink lets each system be balanced on its own. Gating the rare currencies on successful clears per weapon per Heat level pushes players to rotate weapons and raise difficulty, rather than grind one favourite on easy settings.

### The Pact turns difficulty into a self-paced, rewarded ladder

**Measured.** The Pact of Punishment unlocks after the first escape. It has **15 conditions worth 63 Heat**, plus a 16th in Hell Mode for 64 ([Hades Wiki: Pact of Punishment](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment)). The conditions are modular rather than a single slider: foes +20% damage per rank, healing −25% per rank, +20% foes, new boss techniques, a timer per region, and so on. **Routine Inspection disables Mirror talents three at a time**, so a fully upgraded player can switch the meta power back off for Heat. Each weapon has its own target Heat. Each clear at or above it pays bounties and raises the target by 1, and only the lowest uncompleted rung pays. Heat above 20 pays nothing but cosmetic statues at 8, 16 and 32 ([Hades Wiki: Pact of Punishment](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment)). God Mode is a separate accessibility track. It gives 20% damage resistance and **+2% per death**, up to 80%. It can be toggled at any time and does not lock content ([Hades Wiki: God Mode](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/God_Mode)).

**Developer statement.** Kasavin credits Slay the Spire's Ascension as the Pact's inspiration. He calls Supergiant's mantra "every run counts", with "many channels of permanent progression", and says they avoided "daily login bonuses" because they "were not interested in making it addictive" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/features/hades-exclusive-interview-supergiant)). On easy modes he says "if you could just blow through it, what's interesting about the game goes away", and that difficulty settings "may need to be proprietary to the game" ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-god-mode-interview)). Hades II goes further toward sidegrades. Its **25 Arcana cards** cost 56 Grasp in total, but the Grasp cap tops out at 30, so the player can never equip everything ([Hades Wiki: Arcana Cards](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Arcana_Cards)). Its bounties become weapon-locked Testaments at fixed Fear thresholds ([Hades Wiki: Oath of the Unseen](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Oath_of_the_Unseen)).

## Dead Cells and Risk of Rain 2 keep stat power near zero

### Dead Cells: find-then-pay unlocks, capped comfort upgrades, healing-stripping difficulty

**Measured.** Cells are Dead Cells' single meta currency. They are spent at the Collector between biomes and **lost on death if unspent**. A beaten run carries leftovers forward in a bag, plus 40 bonus cells ([deadcells.wiki.gg: Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells)). Unlocking takes two steps. A blueprint must first drop and be carried alive to the next biome. Drop rates are 100%, 10%, 1.7%, 0.4% or 0.03% by tier, across **256 enemy and boss blueprints, 42 secret blueprints and 2 more from the 5 BSC boss**. Then the blueprint is bought with cells ([deadcells.wiki.gg: Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)). Permanent power is small and capped. Health Flask ranks I–IV cost **505 cells for 4 charges**. Gold Reserves I–V cost 740 cells to keep up to 2,500 gold. Recycling costs 145 cells. There are **no HP or damage upgrades**. The large sink is the Legendary Forge: **13,500 cells to raise item-quality odds** ([deadcells.wiki.gg: Runes and upgrades](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades); [Legendary Forge](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Legendary_Forge)). Several upgrades require N items unlocked (10, 25, 30, 40), which forces pool growth.

Boss Stem Cells are the ascension ladder, earned one level per final-boss win. Their main lever is **removing healing**: fountains in every Passage at 0 BSC, then every other Passage, then one flask refill, then three per run, then none. 4 BSC adds enemies that teleport to the player, and 5 BSC adds Malaise. Higher BSC also pays more: **×2 cells at 2–3 BSC and ×3 at 4–5**, plus about 66 blueprints that only drop on higher BSC ([deadcells.wiki.gg: Boss Stem Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells); [Blueprints](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Blueprints)). On Steam, **40.3% of owners have beaten the Hand of the King**, 70.5% have unlocked 10 weapons and 45.8% have unlocked 10 skills ([Steam achievements](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/588650/achievements)).

Players complained about pool dilution: they had to unlock items they did not want to reach thresholds and better items ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/3/1621724915796861021)). The developers responded with opt-out curation, not optional unlocks. Custom Mode in 1.1 added item-pool toggles. A later note says items "can be locked back in the Collector UI". **Achievements switch off below 20 enabled items** ([deadcells.wiki.gg: 1.1](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1); [2.3](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/2.3); [Custom Mode](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Custom_Mode)). Assist Mode (2.9) can cut enemy HP and damage to 20% **without disabling achievements** ([deadcells.wiki.gg: Assist Mode](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Assist_Mode)). **Developer statement.** Lead developer Sébastien Bénard wanted the character's strength "being equal to the extent of your skills", not "super overpowered", and said the metagame's role is "a meaningful sensation of progression" when a run ends early ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2018/01/25/gorgeous-rogue-lite-action-platformer-dead-cells-is-battling-onto-ps4-this-year)).

### Risk of Rain 2: about 205 challenges, zero stat bonuses

**Measured.** RoR2's whole persistent layer is about **205 challenges**: 14 unlock survivors, 30 items, 14 equipment, 65 skills, 62 skins and 20 artifacts. None grants a stat bonus ([riskofrain2.wiki.gg: Challenges](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Challenges)). Two of 20 survivors are available at the start ([Survivors](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Survivors)). By the researcher's tally, only about 13% of items start locked. The challenges teach first and test later. Early ones reward basic acts, such as completing a teleporter, dying 5 times ("Learning Process" → Tougher Times) or finding 10 white items. Middle ones reward secrets and cumulative play (30 stages, 3,000 kills). Late ones reward Monsoon clears and skill feats. Alternate-skill challenges are survivor-specific feats, such as Mercenary staying airborne for 30 s ([riskofrain2.wiki.gg: Challenges](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Challenges)).

Artifacts are opt-in run modifiers (Glass: 500% damage, 10% HP; Honor: elites only; Command: choose your items). They unlock through **9-symbol codes found in the world and a trial in Bulwark's Ambry**. In multiplayer, **unlocks are pooled and any member's artifacts can be used** ([riskofrain2.wiki.gg: Artifacts](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Artifacts)). Lunar Coins are the only persistent currency. Each kill has a **0.5% drop chance, which halves after every drop within a run**. Fixed sources pay 5–10 coins, and challenges pay 1–10 on first completion (a Seekers of the Storm addition). Coins buy only run-scoped options: Lunar items, rerolls, stage choice, and Artificer's one-time 10-coin unlock ([riskofrain2.wiki.gg: Lunar Coin](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Lunar_Coin); [Bazaar](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Bazaar_Between_Time)). The long tail is **Eclipse 1–8**, stacking modifiers per survivor whose only reward is a cosmetic icon. In co-op, the lobby's level is the lowest among its survivors ([riskofrain2.wiki.gg: Eclipse](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Eclipse)). Completionist time is about **145 h** by HowLongToBeat-attributed figures, against about 10.5 h for one win ([Plat Prices](https://platprices.com/en-bg/game/401-risk-of-rain-2)).

**Developer statement.** Paul Morse, on the series: "We didn't want to gate 'all the fun content' behind unlocks". Later unlocks "weren't strictly power upgrades, but oftentimes more complex game mechanics". Each unlock was checked so it would not "make other items obsolete", and they were "very careful not to dilute the pool" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/risk-of-rain-interview)). The Hopoo team said "skill takes a front seat" in RoR2 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-moving-from-2d-to-3d-shaped-the-design-of-i-risk-of-rain-2-i-)). No RoR2-specific quote rejecting stat meta was found.

The two stat-shop comparators both cap power through **escalating global prices**, not hard caps. In Vampire Survivors, every rank bought adds a fee of floor(20 × 1.1^n) to every later purchase. That fee makes up about **92% of the 42,965,380 gold** needed to buy everything. Vampire Survivors also sells difficulty in the same shop (Curse, +10% enemy speed, HP and quantity per rank) and offers a full refund ([VS Wiki: PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps)). **58.4% of owners survived 20 minutes with at least +10% Curse**, so a majority chose to make the game harder ([Steam API](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamUserStats/GetGlobalAchievementPercentagesForApp/v0002/?gameid=1794680&format=json)).

Optional ladders hold a large share of winners. **Brotato keeps 84–90% of winners from each Danger level to the next, and half of all Danger 0 winners (36.5 of 72.8) clear Danger 5.** Rogue Legacy 2 converts about 58% of final-boss winners to an NG+ Prime kill. Gungeon converts about 59% of Fifth Chamber winners to all four Pasts. Slay the Spire's 20-step Ascension holds only about 11% of the players who unlocked it through A20 (7.3 / 66.3). All figures are from the Steam API links above. **Inference.** Coarse ladders of about 5 steps hold far more players than a fine 20-step ladder, though genre and run length confound this comparison. The games with the least power-granting meta, Brotato and Slay the Spire, also post the highest first-win shares. The heaviest structural or grind meta, Rogue Legacy 2 and Gungeon, posts the lowest. That contradicts any simple claim that stat meta gets more players to the ending. It does not prove the reverse, because base difficulty and game length differ widely. No published retention study comparing stat meta with no stat meta was found, and the anti-stat-meta position in design writing rests on felt skill expression and grind aversion, not data ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-roguelite-meta-progression); [PC Gamer on Dodge Roll](https://pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/as-enter-the-gungeon-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-its-creators-have-some-choice-words-for-the-modern-roguelike-were-seeing-it-mutate-to-the-version-of-itself-that-popularity-obfuscates)).

## Pressure: a 326-point stat tree and a 9% starting pool, measured from code

### The Mastery tree is wide, flat and slow at bot pace

**Measured.** Re-implementing `_build_tree` gives **212 nodes in 17 branches, 326 points to complete**. 134 nodes cost 1, 42 cost 2 and 36 cost 3 ([mastery.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/mastery.gd)). Fully bought, the tree gives **+21% damage, +12% fire rate, +7 pp crit, +32 max HP (about +32% on the 100 base), +6 armour, +1 projectile**, plus +11% damage and +12% potency for each of 12 elements. These bonuses are summed additively into `Player.stat` ([player.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/player.gd)). **The 12 element branches hold 96 nodes and 120 points, 37% of the tree**, and they do nothing until that element's guns are in the pool. Points come only at run end: `floor + floor(kills/150) + 3 if won`. **One floor is worth 150 kills**, so a floor-1 death pays a flat 1 point however well the player fought. The bot's median run earns 1 point, so the tree takes **about 286–326 runs, or 7–8 hours at 41.7–45.9 points per hour**. A hypothetical cleared run with 2,166 kills would earn 22 points, which would cut completion to about 15 runs; no bot has cleared ([mastery.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/mastery.gd); [autotest logs](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/autotest)). The GDD calls mastery "the one deliberate exception to 'nothing makes a run easier'" ([GDD.md](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/GDD.md)).

**Inference.** Compared with Hades, the total power is similar in kind: flat HP and damage. The shape differs. Hades front-loads big, legible survival ranks (a revive for 30 Darkness), puts its long cost tail on agency, and makes every slot a choice. Pressure spreads the power across 212 nodes of +1–3% with no exclusive choices, and its biggest single effects (+1 projectile, +2 pierce, +2 chain jumps) are cost-3 leaves deep in the branches. Measured against Dead Cells and RoR2, the 32% HP gain alone is more permanent power than either game sells.

### Unlocks gate the reaction system that defines the game

**Measured.** 55 achievements grant 61 unlocks: 39 guns, 12 classes, 5 obstacles, 2 rarity tiers, 2 shop upgrades and 1 room type. A new save opens **4 of 43 guns (9%), 1 of 13 classes, and 6 of 67 unlock ids**. Gear up to Rare is open, 183 of 238 items (77%) ([progress.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/progress.gd); [items.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/items.gd)). The four base guns cover wet, shock, fire and oil, which pair directly into **5 of the 65 reactions** ([elements.gd](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/scripts/elements.gd)). 32 of the 55 achievements count one specific reaction, including all 12 class unlocks (60–90 signature reactions each). A Monte Carlo over 4,000 simulated saves, resampled from the 7 current-build bot runs, gives a **median of 2 achievements after run 1, 11 by run 5, 18 by run 10 and 30 by run 50, plateauing at 32**. Holy, the last element, arrives at a median run of about 45. **23 achievements never unlock at bot skill**: everything gated on floors, bosses or wins, plus off-element reactions ([autotest logs](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/autotest)). Reaction-count goals are highly volatile. One fire-plus-oil opening produced 142 Infernos in 75 s, enough for Pyromancer on run 1, while the median run produced 4. Means and medians differ 5–10× for Inferno, Steam and Plasma.

**Inference.** RoR2 locks about 13% of items and Morse says not to gate "the fun content". Pressure locks 91% of its guns, and guns carry the elements that the reaction system depends on, so a new player sees only a small slice of the game's core. The design comment in `progress.gd` ("the first four land in a first run") is roughly met from run 2 on, but the first run lands about 2, because Tourist and Regular cannot land in run 1.

## Evidence limits: bot data only, n=7, and no co-op

The Pressure numbers describe a scripted, kiting Vagrant with no mastery, a wiped save every run, and time caps set by the tester. **In the current-build set, 3 of 7 runs were time-capped and none completed floor 1.** Only 4 runs have telemetry, with 0, 3, 0 and 0 elite kills and no bosses ([autotest logs](C:/Users/emilm/Documents/pressure/autotest); [runs.jsonl](C:/Users/emilm/AppData/Roaming/Godot/app_userdata/Pressure/telemetry/runs.jsonl)). Kills per minute (about 60) is the only rate stable across samples. Per-run totals reflect the tester's caps. The Monte Carlo assumes a constant pool, so it cannot show the feedback loop where new guns open new reactions, and it says nothing about floor, boss or win unlocks. Every telemetry record has `players: 1`, so co-op pacing, how unlocks are shared, and lobby difficulty are entirely unmeasured. On the comparison side, Steam percentages are owner-denominated snapshots. Hades and RoR2 have no first-win proxy in these notes. Developer intent statements are thin for Dead Cells and RoR2: no postmortem explains cell loss or the absence of stats. Every "runs to X" figure for Pressure is therefore a statement about the bot, not about players, and the recommendations below are ranked by how little they depend on unknown human behaviour.

R1–R3 and R10 are corrections that need no human data and should come first. R4–R8 are the structural rework. Under R7 and R8, all pacing targets should be treated as provisional until R2 produces the first real-player runs. The current tree's 7–8 hours of bot time to completion could be far too slow or far too fast for humans, and a cleared run alone would cut it to about 15 runs. R9, R11 and R12 extend the game's lifespan and matter most once players start winning, which bot data cannot yet show.

## Conclusion

Pressure's meta layer is miscalibrated in a specific way: it is generous where the reference games are stingy and stingy where they are generous. It hands out hundreds of tiny permanent stat nodes, which Dead Cells and RoR2 refuse to sell and Hades sells only as cheap, swappable sidegrades. At the same time it withholds roughly 90% of the elements and guns that make its reaction system interesting, the content Morse explicitly says not to gate. The cross-game data suggests the long-term engine is not a stat tree but a coarse opt-in difficulty ladder with capped rewards: about half of Brotato's winners climb all five Danger levels. Pressure has no such ladder in its audited meta layer.

The most important finding about the evidence itself is that Pressure cannot yet measure its own meta. Bots skip mastery, telemetry cannot upload, the death room's reactions vanish from the data, and several achievement descriptions misreport their own goals. The rework should start by making the system honest and observable, then reshape it toward options. Each pacing number should be re-measured against the first human runs before it is treated as a target.
